\begin{abstract}
Legal question answering must retrieve evidence that is relevant, complete in
its statutory context, valid at the relevant time, and traceable to an
authoritative source. We evaluate L\_RAG, a Vietnamese legal
Retrieval-Augmented Generation pipeline that combines E5 dense retrieval,
BM25, a knowledge-graph overlay, rank fusion, cross-encoder reranking, and
citation-grounded generation. Across a 500-question benchmark, the ablations
yield three practical findings. Adding metadata to embeddings improves
document-level coverage, but not exact-chunk ranking. The tested hybrid and
graph configurations reduce evidence coverage, so their additional complexity
is not justified under the current settings. In contrast, every tested
cross-encoder improves ranking when candidate evidence is held fixed: Jina
achieves the strongest retrieval scores, BGE the highest lexical
answer-overlap scores, and mMiniLM the lowest reranking latency. A separate,
fixed-context prompt comparison finds that GPT-4o-mini chain-of-thought
prompting lowers lexical overlap, improves only template-based answerability
diagnostics, and adds latency. Because the study has no human judgments of
legal correctness or citation faithfulness, its conclusions are deliberately
limited to retrieval, lexical overlap, abstention diagnostics, and recorded
latency.
\end{abstract}
