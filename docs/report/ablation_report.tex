\begin{abstract}
Legal question answering must retrieve evidence that is relevant, complete in
its statutory context, valid at the relevant time, and traceable to an
authoritative source. We evaluate L\_RAG, a Vietnamese legal
retrieval-augmented generation pipeline that combines E5 dense retrieval,
BM25, a knowledge-graph overlay, reciprocal-rank fusion, second-stage
reranking, and citation-grounded generation. On a fixed 500-question
benchmark (400 answerable and 100 unanswerable questions), we report five
controlled ablation analyses grouped into four run families rather than a
pooled factorial comparison: embedding text; dense, hybrid, and graph
retrieval as one joint retrieval/graph run family; reranking within a shared
candidate pool; and Base versus chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting. Metadata
improves document-level success at depth 10 by 4.5 percentage points but
reduces exact-chunk MRR. The tested BM25+RRF hybrid loses 5.98 Recall@10
points to dense retrieval; its recorded median total rises from 5.483 to
31.538 seconds under an accounting convention that includes amortized BM25
precomputation and is not online request latency. One-hop graph expansion
loses 11.81--12.40 Recall@10 points, chiefly by replacing seed evidence. In a
common 30-candidate pool, Jina
reranking improves Recall@10 by 9.23 points and nDCG@10 by 11.85 points over
RRF-only. Under fixed context, CoT decreases GPT-4o-mini Token F1 by 1.57
points. Retrieval is scored against gold chunk identifiers; lexical answer
overlap, template-based answerability behavior, structural citation fields,
and recorded latency are separate diagnostics. These are benchmark-bounded
engineering findings: they do not establish legal correctness, citation
entailment, or faithful reasoning.
\end{abstract}
