Identifiers are stable across pipeline stages. A chunk resolves to one provision and document, while prediction records join to benchmark questions through \texttt{qa\_id} and to experimental conditions through \texttt{run\_id}. Ground-truth evidence is represented by document, provision, and chunk identifier arrays rather than by a text span alone.

For unanswerable questions, the gold evidence arrays are empty by construction. Prediction records retain failed or skipped statuses in the evaluation ledger, whereas answer-overlap metrics use only valid successful answerable rows. These conventions define the joins and evaluation denominators used in Sections~\ref{sec:implementation-setup} and~\ref{sec:ablation-study}.

\section{Example Documents, Provisions, Chunks, and Edges}
\label{app:example-objects}

The following compact example shows how one source is represented without
including a full legal document:

{\scriptsize
\begin{verbatim}
document: doc_id=LAW-EXAMPLE-2024
          type=law, effective_date=2024-01-01
provision: provision_id=LAW-EXAMPLE-2024-A15
           article=15, parent=LAW-EXAMPLE-2024
chunk: chunk_id=LAW-EXAMPLE-2024-A15-02
       sequence=2, text="..."
edges: DOCUMENT_HAS_PROVISION -> A15
       PROVISION_HAS_CHUNK -> A15-02
       CHUNK_NEXT: A15-01 -> A15-02 -> A15-03
\end{verbatim}
}

The visible passage can be shortened for a prompt, but its parent identifiers
remain unchanged. A relation to an unavailable instrument is represented as
an external stub rather than a fabricated document:

{\scriptsize
\begin{verbatim}
external_stub: stub_id=EXT-8841
               reference="Instrument X, Article 4"
relation: LAW-EXAMPLE-2024 --CITES--> EXT-8841
status: unresolved_target=true
        retrievable_text=false
\end{verbatim}
}

These examples make the graph and retrieval joins explicit. They also explain
why a high-ranked document can be counted as a document-level success while a
specific article or chunk remains absent from the retrieved evidence.

\section{Graph Relationship Vocabulary}
\label{app:graph-vocabulary}

This appendix fixes the interpretation of every edge used by L\_RAG. Structural
edges are created from the document hierarchy, whereas cross-document edges are
normalized from the relationship source. A cross-document record has the
following auditable shape:

{\scriptsize
\begin{verbatim}
edge_id
src_id, dst_id
rel_canonical, rel_group, rel_raw
direction_normalized, direction_verified
external_target, edge_quality_flags[]
provenance{doc_id, other_doc_id, relationship}
\end{verbatim}
}

The identity key is the full triple
\texttt{(src\_id, dst\_id, rel\_canonical)}. Consequently, two documents may
legitimately be connected by several distinct relationships. The raw Vietnamese
label is retained in \texttt{rel\_raw}; \texttt{rel\_canonical} is its
one-to-one normalized form and is materialized as an upper-snake-case graph
type. No canonical label is merged or discarded merely because another label
describes its inverse.

\subsection{Source, confidence, and exclusion policy}

Structural relations inherit the deterministic identities and order fields in
\texttt{documents.jsonl}, \texttt{provisions.jsonl}, and
\texttt{chunks.jsonl}. Cross-document relations originate in
\texttt{edges.jsonl}; \texttt{provenance} preserves the original document pair
and relationship label so every graph hop can be audited. The v2 reconciliation
retains 883,256 normalized edges and quarantines 14,634 rows from 897,890 raw
relationships. Quarantine is loss-preserving: rejected rows remain inspectable
with their exclusion reasons.

The canonical \texttt{GraphEdge} does not invent a scalar confidence score.
Confidence is represented by observable controls: the
\texttt{direction\_verified} gate, \texttt{edge\_quality\_flags}, source
provenance, and corpus-level quality reports. A normalized but unverified edge
may remain in the audit data, but it cannot enter a reasoning path. References
to missing in-corpus targets become deterministic \texttt{ExternalStub} nodes
with \texttt{citation\_safe=false}; they preserve connectivity but cannot be
returned as answer evidence.

The builder consequently exposes two views: all parsed document edges for
audit and \texttt{verified\_document\_edges} for reasoning. Duplicate node
identifiers are rejected, orphan provisions/chunks and missing stub targets
are surfaced as build warnings, and structural-edge counts are reported by
type. Query-time traversal must use the verified view; filtering an unsafe edge
after generation would be too late because it may already have influenced the
answer context.

\subsection{Validity and authority conventions}

Validity is temporal rather than a permanently stored graph label. Each row in
\texttt{validity\_timeline.jsonl} records
\texttt{id\_str}, \texttt{event\_type}, \texttt{event\_date\_iso},
\texttt{counterparty\_id}, \texttt{scope}, \texttt{rel\_canonical},
\texttt{source\_edge\_id}, and \texttt{direction\_verified}. Event types cover
enactment, effectiveness, expiry, replacement, amendment, suspension, and their
partial variants. Partial events retain an article-level or free-text
\texttt{scope} instead of collapsing the whole document into one status.

At query time, \texttt{currency\_status(id\_str, as\_of\_date)} can fold
verified events whose effective dates do not exceed an explicitly supplied
date. In the current implementation, callers must pass an ISO-formatted
\texttt{as\_of\_date} for this guarantee: passing \texttt{None} labels the
overlay ``today'' but does not exclude future-dated events. This mismatch is a
known implementation limitation, and the reported graph ablation does not
claim temporal-applicability evaluation. Every event must trace to a
\texttt{source\_edge\_id}. The stored \texttt{currency\_hint} is a
non-authoritative fallback when the timeline is incomplete, and an unresolved
case remains \texttt{unknown}. Authority is orthogonal to currency:
\texttt{authority\_index.jsonl} maps document type to a versioned
\texttt{legal\_authority\_rank}, where smaller values denote higher authority
and 99 denotes an unknown type. Authority, recency, and validity are therefore
available as separate, inspectable ranking signals.

The implemented fold sorts date strings with deterministic tie-break fields,
discards events whose direction is unverified, and, when an explicit date is
provided, ignores later events. ISO-date validation and treatment of undated
events must therefore be enforced upstream. The coarse mapping is:
\texttt{enacted}/\texttt{effective}
to \texttt{active}; \texttt{expired}/\texttt{replaced} to \texttt{expired};
amendment and partial-expiry/replacement events to \texttt{partial}; and whole
or partial suspension to \texttt{suspended}. With no eligible event, or with
an unrecognized final event, the result is \texttt{unknown}. This fold is a
retrieval overlay, not a substitute for legal interpretation of a provision's
scope.

For example, consider a hypothetical document with an \texttt{effective}
event dated 2020-01-01 and a verified \texttt{partially\_amended} event dated
2023-07-01 whose scope is ``Điều 5.'' A query as of 2022-12-31 receives
\texttt{active}; a query as of 2024-01-01 receives \texttt{partial}, while the
scope remains available to explain why the document must not be treated as
wholly expired. If the amendment edge is unverified, it cannot change the
derived status. When multiple authority-index entries match a document type,
the implementation selects the lowest rank and then the newest version among
equal-rank entries; absence of a match yields rank 99 with an explicit fallback
source.

\section{Prompt and Output Formats}
\label{app:prompt-output-formats}

This appendix records the observable contract between retrieval, generation,
and evaluation. The primary Base--reasoning comparison fixes the model,
retrieved context and order, decoding parameters, and 1,024-token output budget;
only \texttt{prompt\_strategy} changes. Both conditions use prompt template
\texttt{legal-grounded-answer-v2-citations} and citation contract
\texttt{retrieved-context-citations-v1}.

\subsection{Common prompt envelope}

The following is the common, human-readable prompt envelope. Braced fields are
filled by the evaluator, and the retrieved passages are treated as evidence,
not as further instructions. It consolidates repository-visible instructions;
because the frozen manifests retain prompt hashes rather than the complete
rendered strings, it is not presented as a byte-for-byte reconstruction. The
hashes in Table~\ref{tab:appendix-prompt-identity} are the authoritative frozen
identities.

{\scriptsize
\begin{verbatim}
Bạn là hệ thống RAG pháp lý Việt Nam.
Chỉ trả lời dựa trên CONTEXT được cung cấp.
Không suy diễn, không bịa thêm ngoài CONTEXT.
Nếu CONTEXT không đủ thông tin để trả lời, hãy trả lời:
"Không có đủ thông tin trong ngữ cảnh được cung cấp."

QUESTION:
{question}
ANSWER_TYPE:
{answer_type}
CONTEXT:
{ranked_context}
Trả lời bằng tiếng Việt có dấu:
\end{verbatim}
}

The Base strategy then requests a concise final answer, grounded in the supplied
passages, with bracketed citations whenever the context contains a legal basis.
The reasoning strategy adds the observable instruction to analyze the supplied
evidence before producing the final answer and to attach citations to supported
claims. This is a prompt intervention, not evidence that a model's hidden
reasoning is available or faithful. The frozen ablation artifacts contain final
answers and output diagnostics, but no raw chain-of-thought, structured
justifications, reasoning-token counts, or reasoning cost.

\subsection{Context serialization and citations}

Each retrieved chunk is serialized with a stable numeric marker and its legal
identity. The implementation uses the citation anchor when present, otherwise
the citation label, provision identifier, or chunk identifier in that order:

{\scriptsize
\begin{verbatim}
[rank] chunk_id=<chunk_id>;
       provision_id=<provision_id>;
       document_id=<document_id>;
       citation=<citation_anchor>
<chunk_text>
\end{verbatim}
}

The answer cites evidence with \texttt{[rank]} markers, for example
\texttt{[2]} or \texttt{[2][5]}. A marker is valid only when it resolves to an
item in the supplied context. The stored citation object preserves
\texttt{document\_id}, \texttt{provision\_id}/\texttt{section},
\texttt{chunk\_id}, rank, source path, retrieval score, and the answer offsets
of each marker. This makes provision-level citation display reproducible even
when several chunks originate from the same instrument.

A schematic two-passage context illustrates the binding between rank markers
and stable corpus identities:

{\scriptsize
\begin{verbatim}
[1] chunk_id=DOC-A::article::5::chunk::1;
    provision_id=DOC-A::article::5;
    document_id=DOC-A;
    citation=Văn bản A, Điều 5
<text of the first retrieved chunk>

[2] chunk_id=DOC-B::article::2::chunk::1;
    provision_id=DOC-B::article::2;
    document_id=DOC-B;
    citation=Văn bản B, Điều 2
<text of the second retrieved chunk>
\end{verbatim}
}

The numeric marker is local to one prompt and must not be interpreted as a
global source identifier. The stable join path is marker $\rightarrow$ context
rank $\rightarrow$ \texttt{chunk\_id} $\rightarrow$
\texttt{provision\_id} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{document\_id}. Reordering the
same chunks therefore changes marker meaning, which is why the fixed-context
ablation verifies the complete ordered top-10 chunk-ID sequence, not merely
the set of retrieved documents.

\textbf{Answer type} & \textbf{Visible answer shape} & \textbf{Evaluation convention} \\
\midrule
\texttt{boolean} & First non-empty line is exactly \textit{Có} or \textit{Không}; supporting text follows \textit{Giải thích:} and carries citations. & Exact match is computed on the first answer line; faithfulness and citation checks use the complete response. \\
\texttt{extractive} & Short Vietnamese span or proposition recoverable from the supplied passage, followed by supporting marker(s). & Token F1 and ROUGE-L compare the final answer with the reference; citations are parsed separately. \\
\texttt{abstractive} & Concise Vietnamese synthesis of the supplied evidence, with markers adjacent to supported factual claims. & Lexical answer metrics and citation diagnostics are both reported; neither alone establishes legal correctness. \\
\texttt{unanswerable} & Exact visible refusal: \textit{``Không có đủ thông tin trong ngữ cảnh được cung cấp.''} & The evaluator normalizes the refusal into the semantic insufficient-context decision and scores answerability accuracy. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table*}

An empty context is handled before generation and recorded as a skipped or
insufficient-context case. A non-empty but inadequate context must trigger the
same refusal rather than a guessed answer. The semantic state may be represented
internally as \texttt{INSUFFICIENT\_CONTEXT}; it is the Vietnamese sentence
above, not that internal token, that forms the visible response contract.

Representative valid surfaces are:

{\scriptsize
\begin{verbatim}
boolean:
Có
Giải thích: CONTEXT nêu điều kiện áp dụng tại Điều 5 [1].

extractive/abstractive:
Điều kiện áp dụng gồm ... [1][2].

unanswerable:
Không có đủ thông tin trong ngữ cảnh được cung cấp.
\end{verbatim}
}

For Boolean scoring, only the first non-empty line is used for Exact Match,
but citations, refusal detection, and other diagnostics inspect the complete
answer. For extractive and abstractive cases, Token F1 and ROUGE-L operate on
the normalized final text; they do not validate that a cited source entails
the claim. A refusal containing extra unsupported legal advice violates the
intent of the refusal contract even if the expected phrase is present.

\subsection{Saved prediction record and diagnostic limits}

The evaluator stores one auditable record per question. The abbreviated schema
below separates the generated text, supplied evidence, parsed citations, and
execution state:

{\scriptsize
\begin{verbatim}
qa_id, question, category, difficulty, answer_type
reference_answer, predicted_answer, ground_truth
metrics{exact_match, token_f1, rouge_l,
        answerability_decision_accuracy, context_recall@k}
retrieved_context[{rank, document_id, provision_id,
                   chunk_id, text, score, citation_anchor}]
citations[{citation_id, document_id, section, chunk_id,
           rank, score, source_path}]
citation_references[{marker, answer_start, answer_end}]
citation_metrics{citation_count, valid_citation_count,
                 invalid_citation_count,
                 unique_cited_source_count,
                 structural_citation_coverage}
status, failed_stage, error, latency_ms
\end{verbatim}
}

The status fields make incomplete runs distinguishable from low-quality
answers. An empty retrieved context is intercepted before model invocation;
provider or serialization errors populate \texttt{failed\_stage} and
\texttt{error}; a successfully parsed response receives \texttt{success} even
when its lexical score is low. Scheduled-denominator reliability therefore
counts failures separately, while paired lexical analysis requires successful
answerable rows in both conditions.

The response parser can detect a provider-supplied reasoning field or remove a
\texttt{<think>...</think>} block before scoring the final answer; otherwise it
records \texttt{reasoning\_source=not\_returned}. For the frozen ablation, only
the final-answer surface and observable diagnostics are analyzed. Citation
presence, marker validity, unique-source count, and structural coverage measure
output structure and parser behavior. They do not prove that a cited provision
entails the adjacent claim, that all controlling law was retrieved, or that an
abstention was substantively correct.

Reasoning extraction follows a fixed precedence: a dedicated provider field,
then a complete \texttt{<think>...</think>} block, then
\texttt{not\_returned}. An unterminated think block is not guessed or silently
repaired. This parser behavior prevents visible reasoning wrappers from
contaminating the scored final answer when a supported response shape is
available, but it does not establish that the extracted text is faithful to
the model's internal computation.

\section{Web Demo Interface}
\label{app:web-demo}

The L\_RAG prototype includes a web interface for submitting Vietnamese legal questions, inspecting grounded answers, tracing citations to their supporting passages, and handling cases where the available evidence is insufficient. This appendix illustrates the main user-facing interaction states of the prototype.

Figure~\ref{fig:web-demo-landing} shows the initial interface. The user can enter a legal question directly or select one of the suggested examples. The sidebar provides access to the interaction history, Production/Demo mode selection, usage guidance, and configurable retrieval settings. The retrieval scope can also be selected before submitting a question.

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{figures/landing.png}
\caption{Initial LexVN interface for submitting a Vietnamese legal question. The sidebar exposes session controls, Production/Demo mode, guidance, and retrieval settings, while the main panel provides the query input and suggested questions.}
\label{fig:web-demo-landing}
\end{figure*}

Figure~\ref{fig:web-demo-answer} illustrates a successful grounded response. The example asks:

\begin{quote}
\textit{``Người lao động được nghỉ phép năm bao nhiêu ngày?''}
\end{quote}

The interface returns a concise answer stating that an employee who has worked for the same employer for 12 months is entitled to 12 working days of annual leave under normal working conditions. The answer contains an inline citation marker, and the corresponding source is displayed below the response with its document and provision information. This design keeps the generated answer and its supporting legal evidence visible in the same interaction.

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{figures/answer.png}
\caption{Grounded-answer view for an annual-leave question. The generated answer includes an inline citation, while the supporting source is displayed below for direct inspection.}
\label{fig:web-demo-answer}
\end{figure*}

Users can inspect a citation in greater detail by opening the source panel.
Figure~\ref{fig:web-demo-source-passage} shows the extracted legal passage supplied as evidence to the answer, while Figure~\ref{fig:web-demo-source-metadata} presents the corresponding document and provenance metadata.
\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{figures/passage.png}
\caption{Extracted legal passage associated with the cited source. The panel exposes the text from Article 111 on annual leave that supports the generated answer.}
\label{fig:web-demo-source-passage}
\end{figure*}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{figures/metadata.png}

\end{figure*}


The interface also exposes explicit insufficient-evidence behavior. Figure~\ref{fig:web-demo-abstention} shows a question asking whether a specific deadline exists for a facility to complete the purchase of fire-prevention equipment:

\begin{quote}
\textit{``Có thời hạn cụ thể để cơ sở hoàn thành việc mua sắm trang thiết bị phòng cháy, chữa cháy không?''}
\end{quote}

When the available evidence does not support a reliable answer, the interface displays the state \textit{``Chưa đủ bằng chứng''} rather than presenting an unsupported legal conclusion. No citation is attached to this response because no supporting passage is available for the requested claim.

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{figures/abstention.png}
\caption{Insufficient-evidence state. When the available sources do not support the requested legal information, the interface explicitly indicates that the evidence is insufficient instead of generating an unsupported answer.}
\label{fig:web-demo-abstention}
\end{figure*}

Together, these interface states expose the main user-facing L\_RAG workflow: submitting a legal question, receiving a grounded response, inspecting the cited passage and its provenance, and receiving an explicit abstention when sufficient evidence is unavailable. The interface therefore makes the distinction between answer generation, evidence inspection, and insufficient-evidence handling visible to the end user.
