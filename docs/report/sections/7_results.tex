\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The results tell a consistent story: better candidate ordering is more useful
than adding retrieval stages under the current settings. The tables below
separate document discovery, exact evidence ranking, answer-overlap diagnostics,
and latency so that a gain in one dimension is not mistaken for a general
quality improvement. The generator-reasoning ablation is reported separately
in Section~\ref{sec:ablation-reasoning}. Retrieval metrics use answerable
questions and are paired within each experiment family. Deltas are reported in
percentage points (pp), not relative percentages.

\subsection{Embedding Representation}
\label{sec:embedding-results}

Adding metadata changes the level at which retrieval succeeds. At top 10,
exact chunk recall is unchanged to one decimal place, while document success
increases by 4.5 pp. At the same time, the paired MRR decrease is reliably
below zero. Metadata therefore helps the system locate the right legal
instrument, but it does not improve---and may delay---the first exact
supporting chunk.

\subsection{Dense, Hybrid, and Graph Retrieval}
\label{sec:retrieval-results}

DenseOnly is the strongest exact-chunk retriever in this family. HybridOnly is
lower on Recall@10 and nDCG@10, and its paired Recall loss has a 95\% interval
entirely below zero. Graph expansion lowers Recall@10 for both seed retrievers:
by 12.40 pp from DenseOnly and 11.81 pp from HybridOnly. The per-query evidence
counts explain the decline: expansion adds a gold chunk for only one DenseOnly
query and six HybridOnly queries, while removing at least one previously
retrieved gold chunk for 61 and 59 queries, respectively.

Graph contexts are denser: ID precision rises from 0.0580 to 0.0800 for Dense
and from 0.0493 to 0.0641 for Hybrid. That gain must be read beside the much
larger recall loss. Recorded unanswerable accuracy also rises (0.38 to 0.41 for
Dense and 0.38 to 0.47 for Hybrid), but paired Token F1 changes are small and
inconclusive. The present graph policy thus creates a precision--coverage
trade-off, not a demonstrated overall improvement.

\label{sec:reranker-results}

every reranker sees the same candidate evidence. Jina achieves the strongest
retrieval scores, BGE the highest lexical-overlap scores, and Qwen3 competitive
quality at a much larger reranker-stage cost. The recorded unanswerable column
is a template-based diagnostic, not semantic legal-answer evaluation.

\subsection{Generation and Efficiency}
\label{sec:generation-quality}

Automatic answer metrics do not establish legal correctness. Metadata changes
Token F1 by +0.1 pp and ROUGE-L by $-0.1$ pp; graph expansion changes Token F1
by approximately +0.4 pp for Dense and +0.2 pp for Hybrid, with bootstrap
intervals crossing zero. In the reranker family, BGE has the strongest observed
overlap scores, while Jina has the best retrieval scores and a lower total p50
than BGE (4.158 s vs. 5.848 s). These results support a conditional engineering
choice, not a claim that either reranker generates more legally correct answers.

Efficiency differences are substantial. Hybrid retrieval raises total median
latency from 5.483 s to 31.538 s and reports a one-time BM25 precomputation
cost of about 13,495 s. Among rerankers, mMiniLM is fastest (0.385 s
reranker-stage p50), Jina is intermediate (1.278 s), BGE is slower (2.872 s),
and Qwen3 is much slower (9.695 s). The reranker artifacts do not record p95,
peak GPU memory, or full-online sparse latency, so those deployment quantities
remain open.
