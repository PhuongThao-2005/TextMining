\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The ablations replace a broad ``more components are better'' assumption with a
more selective conclusion. In the current benchmark and implementation,
components that improve document context do not necessarily improve exact
evidence retrieval, and components that are structurally appealing can still
harm coverage when the context budget is fixed. The strongest result is not a
larger retrieval stack; it is better ranking of a fixed candidate pool.

\subsection{What the Findings Mean}
\label{sec:interpretation}

\paragraph{Metadata changes the level of success.}
Adding document and provision metadata helps the retriever identify the right
legal instrument, but does not improve the rank of the first exact gold chunk.
The header tokens likely provide useful document-level cues while competing
with passage content when the target is a specific provision. The practical
implication is to evaluate both document coverage and chunk ranking: reporting
only one would hide this trade-off.

\paragraph{The current hybrid and graph policies are not default choices.}
Hybrid retrieval underperforms dense retrieval on exact-chunk coverage and
adds a large latency cost. This does not show that BM25 or RRF are inherently
unsuitable for legal retrieval; it identifies a limitation of this index,
Vietnamese tokenization, fusion constant, and candidate budget. Similarly,
one-hop graph expansion increases context density but removes more retrieved
gold evidence than it adds. Under a top-10 budget, the graph is behaving as a
replacement policy, not a safe supplement to high-confidence seed passages.

\paragraph{Reranking is the clearest robust improvement.}
With candidate evidence held fixed, every evaluated cross-encoder improves
Recall@10, MRR@10, and nDCG@10 over RRF-only. Jina has the largest paired
gains and retains substantially more of the available gold evidence, showing
that its advantage comes from promotion rather than access to a larger pool.
This is strong retrieval evidence, although it is still not evidence that the
generator's legal claims are correct.

\paragraph{Reasoning prompts change output behavior, not a validated legal
quality measure.}
For the controlled GPT-4o-mini comparison, CoT lowers lexical overlap across
all evidence-availability strata and increases latency. It reduces
template-detected false refusals and increases citation-marker presence, but
the study does not contain human evaluations of helpfulness, correctness, or
citation entailment. The defensible conclusion is therefore operational: this
CoT prompt has formatting-related benefits but no demonstrated legal-answer
quality advantage.

\subsection{Design Implications}
\label{sec:design-implications}

The results suggest a simple default path for a retrieval-focused deployment:
use dense retrieval followed by a cross-encoder reranker. Jina is the present
quality--latency choice; mMiniLM is the practical fallback when response time
is the main constraint; and BGE is a reasonable alternative when lexical
overlap is prioritized. These are engineering choices for this benchmark, not
rankings of legal reliability.

Hybrid retrieval should be revisited only after measuring online BM25 cost and
tuning tokenization, fusion, and candidate allocation. Graph augmentation
should be introduced as a targeted, evidence-preserving mode for validity or
relationship-heavy questions, rather than as unconditional expansion. A useful
next graph policy would reserve space for high-confidence seed chunks before
adding adjacent or related context.

Regardless of configuration, a production legal system must surface source
citations and validity metadata, abstain when evidence is insufficient, and
direct users to verify the cited instrument's current status. None of the
automatic metrics in this study can certify a legal answer's effect or safety.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

The benchmark has 500 questions, with relatively small multi-hop, hard, and
cross-document slices. Its gold chunk sets may omit passages that a legal
expert would regard as sufficient, and document-level success is not the same
as provision-level correctness. Each ablation family uses one embedding model
and one corpus/index version, but candidate budgets differ across families;
absolute scores should therefore not be pooled across them.

The reranker and reasoning safety analyses rely on deterministic
refusal-marker and template classifiers rather than human assessment. The
reasoning artifacts lack raw CoT, token counts, cost records, semantic claim
labels, and citation-entailment judgments. Four DeepSeek cases also have
different saved context orders across prompts, and the Qwen Base/CoT manifests
differ in code snapshot, timeout, and retry configuration. Those comparisons
remain descriptive.

Finally, cached BM25 candidates mean that reranker latency is not full-online
hybrid latency; p95 latency, peak GPU memory, and controlled throughput are
not available for every configuration. The graph corpus also has unresolved
relationship-direction and validity-annotation questions. We therefore frame
the results as benchmark-bounded engineering evidence, not a claim of legal
reliability or deployment readiness.
