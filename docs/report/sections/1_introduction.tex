\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Legal information systems must do more than return text that appears related
to a question. A useful answer must be grounded in an authoritative
provision, retain the qualifiers and exceptions that shape its meaning, and
respect the provision's temporal status. These requirements are especially
demanding for Vietnamese legal corpora, where a citizen's everyday wording may
differ sharply from statutory language and where an answer can depend on a
provision's place in a legal hierarchy. This report combines the system,
dataset, protocol, and ablation evidence into one traceable account of
L\_RAG.

\subsection{Background and Motivation}
\label{sec:intro-motivation}

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) gives a language model access to
external evidence rather than relying solely on model parameters. In a legal
setting, however, a semantically similar passage is not automatically the
governing rule. A retrieved answer may omit an exception, point to a repealed
instrument, or cite a document without identifying the article and clause
that support the conclusion. The system therefore needs to balance semantic
matching, exact terminology, document structure, and evidence ordering before
generation begins.

For example, a Vietnamese question about whether a land-use transaction is
permitted may use everyday wording, while the controlling provision uses a
formal land category, an effective date, and a cross-reference to another
article. A keyword-only search can miss the paraphrase; a vector-only search
can retrieve a broad discussion; and a generator without source constraints
can produce a fluent but unsupported explanation. L\_RAG treats the answer as
an evidence assembly problem: identify the source document, recover the
relevant provision and neighboring context, rank the available chunks, and
show the citations used to form the response.

\subsection{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:problem-statement}

The study addresses five recurring failure modes in legal RAG:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Vocabulary mismatch.} Users describe a legal problem in
    everyday Vietnamese, whereas statutes use formal terms, identifiers, and
    numeric thresholds. Dense retrieval helps with paraphrase, while lexical
    retrieval can preserve exact legal terminology.
    \item \textbf{Lost statutory context.} Laws are hierarchical---from a
    legal instrument to chapters, articles, clauses, and items. Fixed-size
    chunks can separate a rule from its scope, definition, or exception.
    \item \textbf{Cross-document dependencies.} Provisions can cite, amend,
    replace, or repeal one another. A relevant answer may require a relation
    edge in addition to a similar passage.
    \item \textbf{Temporal and authority errors.} Semantic similarity does
    not determine whether an instrument was effective at the requested date or
    whether a lower-authority rule has been superseded.
    \item \textbf{Unsupported answers.} A model can answer confidently when
    the retrieved context is incomplete. Citation markers and refusal strings
    must be measured separately from legal correctness.
\end{enumerate}

L\_RAG combines dense and sparse retrieval with a graph overlay, a shared
candidate-pool reranker, and citation-oriented prompting. The central
question is not whether every component is plausible in isolation, but which
components improve the evidence available to the generator under a controlled
evaluation protocol.

\subsection{Project Objectives}
\label{sec:objectives}

The project has four operational objectives:

\begin{itemize}
    \item build an end-to-end Vietnamese legal RAG pipeline using dense E5
    retrieval, BM25, reciprocal-rank fusion (RRF), graph expansion,
    reranking, and citation-grounded generation;
    \item represent legal hierarchy, reading order, and document relationships
    in a query-time knowledge-graph overlay;
    \item compare embedding text, retrieval mode, graph policy, reranker, and
    generator prompt choices with question-level pairing; and
    \item evaluate retrieval, answer-overlap, abstention, citation-structure,
    and latency behavior on a 500-question Vietnamese legal benchmark.
\end{itemize}

Each objective has an observable artifact. The corpus and benchmark are
described in Section~\ref{sec:dataset-construction}; the online and offline
modules are specified in Section~\ref{sec:system-design}; frozen manifests
and metric definitions are given in Section~\ref{sec:implementation-setup};
and the five completed comparisons, with inline links to their evidence, are
reported in Section~\ref{sec:ablation-study}.

\subsection{Scope of the System}
\label{sec:scope}

The evaluated scope includes Vietnamese legal-document ingestion,
normalization, provision and chunk construction, relationship processing,
vector and BM25 indexing, graph-based filtering or expansion, candidate
reranking, prompt serialization, generation, and post-hoc scoring. The
benchmark uses stored question IDs, reference answers, and gold document,
provision, and chunk identifiers. The report consequently evaluates a frozen
research pipeline and its saved outputs rather than a live legal service.

The scope excludes autonomous legal advice, formal legal interpretation,
claim-level citation-entailment verification, expert adjudication of legal
correctness, and guaranteed coverage of every current external instrument.
The corpus is a snapshot; unresolved external references are represented as
stubs, and graph direction or validity metadata can require further review.
The generator is instructed to abstain on insufficient context, but an
abstention marker is only an observable output diagnostic. These boundaries
are part of the system definition, not post-hoc qualifications.

\subsection{Main Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

This report contributes a controlled empirical account of L\_RAG rather than
an unqualified claim of legal reliability:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item a structured Vietnamese legal corpus that preserves document,
    provision, chunk, metadata, and relationship provenance;
    \item a G-LRAG graph overlay for containment, reading order, legal
    relations, authority, and validity-aware query filtering;
    \item an end-to-end retrieval and generation pipeline combining E5 dense
    retrieval, optional BM25/RRF, graph expansion, cross-encoder reranking,
    and cited output formatting; and
    \item five paired ablation families that distinguish document discovery
    from exact evidence ranking, isolate graph and reranker effects, and
    separate observable prompt behavior from legal correctness.
\end{enumerate}

The most important methodological contribution is the separation of evidence
availability, answer overlap, refusal behavior, citation structure, and
efficiency. A component is not called an improvement merely because one
aggregate score increases: candidate ceilings, denominators, pairing, and
claim boundaries are reported alongside every headline result.

\subsection{Report Organization}
\label{sec:report-organization}

The report follows a Problem $\rightarrow$ Data $\rightarrow$ Methodology
$\rightarrow$ Implementation $\rightarrow$ Ablations $\rightarrow$
Discussion $\rightarrow$ Conclusion flow. Section~\ref{sec:related-work}
reviews RAG, legal information retrieval, hybrid ranking, GraphRAG, and
evidence-grounded generation. Section~\ref{sec:dataset-construction}
describes the corpus, graph-ready records, benchmark construction, and gold
evidence. Section~\ref{sec:system-design} turns those data products into the
L\_RAG architecture and query workflow. Section~\ref{sec:implementation-setup}
defines frozen artifacts, metrics, pairing, uncertainty, and configurations.
Section~\ref{sec:ablation-study} reports the five controlled families with
clickable table and figure references in the result sentences.
Section~\ref{sec:discussion-limitations-ethics} interprets trade-offs and
sets safety boundaries, while Section~\ref{sec:conclusion-future-work}
summarizes the evidence and prioritizes follow-up experiments. Appendices
record schemas, examples, configuration details, observable prompt formats,
and artifact pointers.
