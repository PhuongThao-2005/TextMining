\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Legal information systems must do more than return text that appears related
to a question. A useful answer must be grounded in an authoritative provision,
retain the qualifiers and exceptions that shape its meaning, and respect the
provision's temporal status. These requirements are especially demanding for
Vietnamese legal corpora, where a citizen's everyday wording may differ sharply
from statutory language and where an answer can depend on a provision's place
in a legal hierarchy.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is a natural starting point: it gives a
language model access to external evidence rather than relying solely on model
parameters. However, a conventional dense-retrieval pipeline leaves several
legal risks unresolved. It can match a query to a broad discussion rather than
the governing clause; fixed-size chunks can separate a rule from its scope or
exception; and semantic similarity alone does not determine whether a cited
instrument remains valid. A legal RAG pipeline therefore needs to balance
semantic matching, exact terminology, document structure, and evidence
ordering before generation begins.

\subsection{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:problem-statement}

This study examines four design choices intended to address those constraints:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Vocabulary mismatch.} Users often describe a legal problem in
    everyday Vietnamese, whereas statutes use formal terms, identifiers, and
    numeric thresholds. Dense retrieval helps with paraphrase, while lexical
    retrieval can preserve exact legal terms.
    \item \textbf{Lost statutory context.} Laws are hierarchical---from a legal
    instrument to chapters, articles, clauses, and items. A retrieved passage
    may be incomplete if its parent provision or an adjacent exception is
    missing.
    \item \textbf{Validity and cross-reference.} Provisions can amend, replace,
    repeal, or cite one another. Retrieval should therefore account for
    authority, temporal status, and explicit legal relationships.
\end{enumerate}

L\_RAG combines dense and sparse retrieval with a graph overlay and a
cross-encoder reranker. The central question is not whether every component is
plausible in isolation, but which components improve the evidence available to
the generator under a controlled evaluation protocol.

\subsection{Objectives}
\label{sec:objectives}

The project has four objectives:

\begin{itemize}
    \item build an end-to-end Vietnamese legal RAG pipeline using dense E5
    retrieval, BM25, reciprocal-rank fusion (RRF), reranking, and
    citation-grounded generation;
    \item represent legal hierarchy, reading order, and document relationships
    in a query-time knowledge-graph overlay;
    \item compare four multilingual rerankers under a shared candidate pool;
    and
    \item evaluate retrieval, answer-overlap, abstention, and latency behavior
    on a 500-question Vietnamese legal benchmark.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

This report contributes a controlled empirical account of L\_RAG rather than
an unqualified claim of legal reliability. Specifically, it provides:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item a modular legal-RAG implementation that supports dense and sparse
    retrieval, graph-based context expansion, reranking, and cited generation;
    \item a large Vietnamese legal corpus and a 500-question benchmark with
    chunk-, provision-, and document-level evidence provenance; and
    \item four paired ablation families that distinguish document coverage from
    exact evidence ranking, isolate reranker effects at a fixed candidate pool,
    and separate observable prompt behavior from legal correctness.
\end{enumerate}
