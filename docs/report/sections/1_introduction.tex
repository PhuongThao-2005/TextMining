\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Legal information access is an evidence-sensitive task: a useful answer must
identify the governing text, preserve the context in which a provision
operates, and allow the reader to inspect its source. These requirements are
especially important for Vietnamese law, where relevant rules are distributed
across laws, decrees, decisions, and circulars connected by hierarchical,
referential, and temporal relationships. This report studies \textbf{L\_RAG},
a Vietnamese legal Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) prototype that
combines corpus structuring, dense and sparse retrieval, a legal knowledge
graph, reranking, and citation-grounded answer generation. The central
question is not whether adding more components makes a pipeline appear more
sophisticated, but which components measurably improve evidence retrieval and
answer behavior under controlled comparisons.

\subsection{Background and Motivation}
\label{sec:background-motivation}

Keyword search remains valuable in legal research when a user already knows
an article number, document title, or exact statutory phrase. In ordinary
question answering, however, users often describe a situation in everyday
Vietnamese rather than reproduce the terminology of an official instrument.
Dense retrieval can bridge this lexical gap by matching semantic content, and
large language models can turn retrieved passages into readable answers.
Neither capability alone is sufficient for dependable legal assistance.

Legal documents are structured and interdependent rather than independent
blocks of text. The meaning of a clause may depend on its article heading,
parent document, neighboring provisions, referenced instruments, or effective
date. A passage that is topically similar can therefore be incomplete,
superseded, or disconnected from the rule needed to answer the question.
Moreover, a fluent generated response can conceal missing evidence or attach
a citation that does not support the adjacent claim. These risks motivate a
pipeline in which retrieval units preserve provenance, graph relations expose
legal structure, and generation is constrained by inspectable evidence.

L\_RAG provides a common implementation and evaluation setting for comparing
these choices. In particular, it enables controlled tests of metadata-enriched
embeddings, dense versus dense--sparse retrieval, one-hop graph expansion,
second-stage rerankers, and base versus explicit reasoning prompts. This
evidence-driven framing is necessary because a component that is intuitively
appropriate for legal text may still reduce exact evidence coverage or impose
an unacceptable latency cost in a fixed pipeline.

\subsection{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:problem-statement}

Given a natural-language Vietnamese legal question $q$, a corpus of legal
documents $\mathcal{D}$, and retrieval units $\mathcal{U}$ derived from those
documents, the system must select a compact evidence set
$E \subset \mathcal{U}$ and generate an answer $a$ whose material claims can
be traced through chunk, provision, and document identifiers. A legal time or
scope constraint can further restrict applicability when the corresponding
overlay is explicitly supplied. The task is difficult because four failure
modes interact:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Lexical mismatch.} A user's vocabulary may differ from the
    formal wording of the applicable provision, which weakens exact keyword
    matching.
    \item \textbf{Structural fragmentation.} Chunking can separate a clause
    from its article header, parent instrument, definitions, exceptions, or
    adjacent provisions, causing retrieval of locally relevant but incomplete
    evidence.
    \item \textbf{Relationship and validity blindness.} Semantic similarity
    alone does not represent citation, amendment, replacement, authority, or
    effective-date relationships between instruments.
    \item \textbf{Ranking and generation error.} Relevant evidence may be
    present in a candidate pool but ranked outside the final context window;
    the generator may then omit support, refuse an answer unnecessarily, or
    produce an unsupported statement.
\end{enumerate}

These failures have practical consequences: the system may cite the wrong
instrument, overlook a controlling provision, present outdated material, or
produce an answer that cannot be audited. The research problem is therefore
to determine how semantic, lexical, structural, and generative components can
be combined while measuring both evidence quality and operational cost. In
this report, success is evaluated through benchmark-bounded retrieval, answer
overlap, answer/refuse behavior, structural citation, completion, and latency
diagnostics; it is not treated as certification of legal correctness.

\subsection{Project Objectives}
\label{sec:objectives}

The project pursues the following measurable objectives, each linked to an
implemented module and a report artifact:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Build traceable retrieval units from Vietnamese legal data.}
    Normalize documents into provisions and chunks with stable document,
    provision, and chunk identifiers and retained metadata. Corpus scale and
    graph integrity are reported in Sections~\ref{sec:dataset-construction}
    and~\ref{sec:dataset-validation}.

    \item \textbf{Implement comparable evidence-retrieval paths.} Provide
    dense E5 retrieval, Vietnamese-tokenized BM25 retrieval, Reciprocal Rank
    Fusion (RRF), and graph-based filtering or expansion behind a shared
    pipeline. Their behavior is measured using Recall@$k$, Hit@$k$, MRR@$k$,
    nDCG@$k$, precision, and latency in
    Sections~\ref{sec:implementation-setup} and~\ref{sec:ablation-study}.

    \item \textbf{Model legal structure and inter-document relations.} Build
    document, provision, and chunk nodes with containment, reading-order, and
    cross-document edges, while retaining provenance and verification gates.
    The schema and traversal role are specified in
    Sections~\ref{sec:system-design} and~\ref{sec:graph-construction}.

    \item \textbf{Improve final-context ranking under a fixed evidence
    ceiling.} Compare RRF-only ordering with mMiniLM, Qwen3, BGE, and Jina
    rerankers over the same 30-candidate pool and measure their ability to
    retain and promote gold evidence into the top 10, together with reranking
    latency.

    \item \textbf{Support grounded generation and explicit abstention.}
    Construct answers from the selected context, expose source markers, and
    instruct the generator to refuse when evidence is insufficient. Generated
    outputs are assessed with lexical-overlap, template-based answerability,
    citation-structure, completion, and latency diagnostics, with their
    limitations stated explicitly.

    \item \textbf{Create a reproducible evaluation basis.} Use a frozen
    500-question benchmark---400 answerable and 100 unanswerable cases---with
    evidence identifiers and saved run artifacts. Paired comparisons and
    bootstrap confidence intervals isolate the tested component within each
    ablation family.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Scope of the System}
\label{sec:scope}

The implemented scope covers the offline preparation of Vietnamese legal
documents, structural parsing into provisions and chunks, dense and sparse
index construction, knowledge-graph construction, candidate retrieval and
fusion, graph-assisted context selection, second-stage reranking, prompt
construction, citation-grounded generation, and offline evaluation. The
experimental scope is limited to the available corpus and frozen benchmark,
the recorded index and graph versions, the tested retrieval budgets, and the
models and prompts documented in Section~\ref{sec:implementation-setup}. The
reported graph ablation tests bounded structural expansion with broad validity
filtering; it does not test the separate query-time temporal-validity or
authority overlay.

The project does not claim exhaustive coverage of Vietnamese law, real-time
synchronization with authoritative legal databases, resolution of every
amendment or relationship-direction ambiguity, or validation of the generated
answers by legal experts. The benchmark is partly synthetic and contains
relatively small multi-hop and cross-document slices. Automatic overlap and
citation-marker metrics do not establish legal correctness, completeness, or
citation entailment. L\_RAG is therefore a research prototype and evaluation
platform, not a source of legal advice or an autonomous decision-making
system. Any deployment affecting rights, obligations, or remedies must verify
the cited text and its current legal status and retain qualified human review.

\subsection{Main Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

This work makes five main contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item A processed Vietnamese legal corpus with document--provision--chunk
    provenance, comprising 151,624 retained document records, 1,386,267
    provisions, and 1,513,376 retrieval chunks.
    \item A typed legal graph containing structural reading-order links and
    883,256 retained cross-document relationship records plus 19,763 external
    stubs, together with quarantine records, a verified-edge view, temporal
    and authority overlay interfaces, and bounded context expansion. The
    overlay interfaces are documented system capabilities, not effects tested
    by the reported graph ablation.
    \item A modular end-to-end pipeline that integrates multilingual dense
    retrieval, Vietnamese BM25, RRF, four second-stage rerankers, and
    evidence-constrained answer generation.
    \item A 500-question Vietnamese legal QA benchmark with answerability,
    category, answer type, reference answer, and document-, provision-, and
    chunk-level evidence provenance.
    \item Five controlled analyses grouped into four run families, separating
    component value from architectural intent. The experiments show that
    metadata primarily improves document-level identification, the tested
    hybrid and graph settings trade away exact evidence coverage, reranking
    provides the clearest retrieval gains, and explicit reasoning prompts do
    not automatically improve generated-answer overlap.
\end{enumerate}

$\rightarrow$ Evaluation $\rightarrow$ Discussion progression.
