\section{Ablation Study}
\label{sec:ablation-study}

We report four completed ablation families. The first isolates the text fed
to the dense encoder, the second compares dense, hybrid, and graph-augmented
retrieval, the third compares six ranking controls over a shared hybrid
candidate pool, and the fourth compares generator prompt modes with the
retrieved context held fixed. Because candidate construction differs between
families, all claims are made within a family; the results are not treated as
one pooled factorial experiment.

\subsection{Embedding Text: Chunk-Only versus Chunk+Metadata}
\label{sec:ablation-chunk-meta}

This comparison uses the same dense encoder, generator, benchmark, seed, and
raw FAISS evaluation path. Only the indexed text changes from the chunk body
to a header-plus-chunk representation. On 400 paired answerable questions,
metadata leaves exact chunk Recall@10 effectively unchanged (48.5\% versus
48.5\%) but increases document-level success from 65.8\% to 70.2\%. The
paired document-success gain is +4.5 percentage points (95\% CI
[+0.5,+8.8]), whereas the MRR@10 change is $-5.9$ points (95\% CI
[$-9.1,-2.7$]). Metadata therefore improves identification of the correct
document while making the first exact gold chunk appear later. This
document--provision divergence motivates reporting both views of retrieval;
the full comparison is in Table~\ref{tab:embedding-results}.

\subsection{Retrieval Mode and Graph Expansion}
\label{sec:ablation-retrieval-mode}

The second family evaluates DenseOnly, HybridOnly, DenseGraph, and
HybridGraph. Hybrid retrieval adds BM25 and RRF to the same dense index;
graph variants expand five seed chunks by one hop and retain at most 10
chunks. Without graph expansion, DenseOnly outperforms HybridOnly on
Recall@10 (48.55\% vs. 42.57\%) and nDCG@10 (0.2813 vs. 0.2638). The paired
Recall@10 difference is $-5.98$ points (95\% CI [$-9.71,-2.21$]).

Graph expansion currently displaces more evidence than it adds. Relative to
DenseOnly, DenseGraph changes Recall@10 by $-12.40$ points and loses at least
one previously retrieved gold chunk for 61 questions while adding a graph-only
gold chunk for only one question. The corresponding HybridOnly to HybridGraph
change is $-11.81$ points, with 59 questions losing a gold chunk and six
gaining one. Graph contexts are denser because they contain fewer chunks on
average (5.605 for DenseGraph and 6.305 for HybridGraph), but their context
recall is lower. The tested graph overlay is thus not yet evidence of a
retrieval-quality improvement.

\subsection{Reranker Choice at a Fixed Candidate Pool}
\label{sec:ablation-reranking}

The third family compares no reranking, RRF-only, mMiniLM, Qwen3, BGE, and
Jina over a reconstructed shared RRF pool of 30 candidates and a final top 10.
All six runs cover the same 500 questions without retrieval or generation
errors, and the reconstruction matches the recorded RRF-only top-10 prefix
for 500/500 questions. Candidate Recall@30 is 0.5995 and Candidate Hit@30 is
0.6450 for every configuration, so the comparison measures ranking and
retention within a fixed evidence ceiling.

Jina is the strongest retrieval configuration: relative to RRF-only, it
improves Recall@10 by +9.23 points, MRR@10 by +13.35 points, and nDCG@10 by
+11.85 points; all three 95\% paired-bootstrap intervals exclude zero. Qwen3
and BGE also have positive intervals, while mMiniLM supplies a smaller but
statistically positive gain at the lowest reranker cost. BGE has the highest
token F1 and ROUGE-L, whereas Jina has the highest observed unanswerable
accuracy. These answer metrics are lexical/abstention diagnostics, not legal
correctness judgments. Table~\ref{tab:reranker-results} reports the aggregate
comparison and Table~\ref{tab:reranker-paired} reports paired effects.

\subsection{Generator Reasoning: Base versus CoT}
\label{sec:ablation-reasoning}

The fourth family evaluates whether explicit chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting
changes answer quality, abstention behavior, citations, or latency when the
retrieved evidence is unchanged. The primary comparison uses the complete
GPT-4o-mini Base and CoT runs: both cover all 500 benchmark questions and
share 400 answerable question IDs. The frozen run artifacts report zero
retrieval-context mismatches, so the paired answer deltas isolate the prompt
mode within the limits of the lexical metrics.

On the 400 paired answerable questions, CoT decreases Token F1 from 24.72\%
to 23.15\% ($-1.57$ percentage points) and ROUGE-L from 21.60\% to 19.90\%
($-1.71$ points). Exact match is essentially unchanged (14.00\% to 13.75\%,
$-0.25$ points). The 95\% paired-bootstrap intervals exclude zero for both
overlap losses; the full paired table is in Table~\ref{tab:llm-reasoning-paired}.
The Token F1 win/tie/loss count is 79/164/157 in favor of Base, consistent
with a broad but modest degradation rather than a universal per-question
failure.

The template-based answerability decision accuracy increases from 82.60\% to
84.00\%. This reflects a lower false-refusal rate on answerable questions
(21.25\% to 19.50\%) while unanswerable recognition remains 98.00\% for both
prompts; it is not a human-validated safety or legal-correctness measure.
CoT also raises citation presence (78.25\% to 81.00\%) and mean unique
sources (1.46 to 1.68), but structural citation coverage falls slightly
(89.42\% to 87.91\%). Generation p50/p95 latency rises from 2.92/5.74 seconds
to 3.68/6.17 seconds, and total p50/p95 rises from 13.99/17.21 to
14.74/18.42 seconds.

Evidence-stratified deltas do not reverse the result: CoT Token F1 is lower
when all gold support is retrieved ($-1.98$ points, $n=138$), when support is
partial ($-1.86$ points, $n=29$), and when support is absent ($-1.30$ points,
$n=233$). The GLM-5 and Qwen3-8B pairs are complete but descriptive. The
DeepSeek CoT cell is incomplete, and four shared successful DeepSeek cases have
different exact top-10 chunk-ID sequences between Base and CoT. The Qwen pair
also uses different code snapshots, timeout values, and retry limits, so its
latency and failure behavior is not a strict apples-to-apples comparison. These
inventory rows and caveats are shown in Table~\ref{tab:llm-reasoning-runs};
incomplete cells are not used for paired claims. The derived report and
machine-readable artifacts are linked from the reproducibility ledger in
Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility}.
