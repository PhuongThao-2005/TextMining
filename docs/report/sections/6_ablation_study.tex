\section{Ablation Study}
\label{sec:ablation-study}

The ablations answer a practical design question: which additions improve the
evidence that reaches the generator, and which merely add cost or complexity?
We evaluate four families: embedding text, retrieval and graph expansion,
reranking, and generator prompting. Candidate construction differs across
families, so comparisons are made only within a family; the study is not a
single pooled factorial experiment.

\subsection{Embedding Text: Chunk-Only versus Chunk+Metadata}
\label{sec:ablation-chunk-meta}

This ablation changes only the text indexed by the dense encoder: either the
chunk body or a header-plus-chunk representation. The encoder, generator,
benchmark, seed, and raw FAISS evaluation path are unchanged. On 400 paired
answerable questions, metadata leaves Recall@10 unchanged (48.5\% for both
conditions) but raises document-level success from 65.8\% to 70.2\%. The
paired gain in document success is +4.5 percentage points (pp; 95\% CI
[+0.5,+8.8]), while MRR@10 falls by 5.9 pp (95\% CI [$-9.1,-2.7$]).

The result separates two levels of retrieval quality. Metadata makes it more
likely that the system enters the correct legal instrument, but it does not
promote the exact supporting chunk earlier in the ranking. It should therefore
be treated as a document-discovery aid, not as an unconditional improvement in
provision-level retrieval. Table~\ref{tab:embedding-results} provides the
complete comparison.

\subsection{Retrieval Mode and Graph Expansion}
\label{sec:ablation-retrieval-mode}

The second family compares DenseOnly, HybridOnly, DenseGraph, and HybridGraph.
Hybrid retrieval adds BM25 and RRF to the same dense index; graph variants
expand five seed chunks by one hop and retain at most 10 chunks. DenseOnly is
the strongest configuration in this family: it exceeds HybridOnly on
Recall@10 (48.55\% vs. 42.57\%) and nDCG@10 (0.2813 vs. 0.2638). The paired
Recall@10 change for HybridOnly is $-5.98$ pp (95\% CI [$-9.71,-2.21$]).

The current graph policy also reduces coverage. Relative to DenseOnly,
DenseGraph lowers Recall@10 by 12.40 pp; it removes at least one previously
retrieved gold chunk for 61 questions and adds a graph-only gold chunk for
only one. Relative to HybridOnly, HybridGraph lowers Recall@10 by 11.81 pp,
with 59 questions losing gold evidence and six gaining it. Graph contexts are
more concentrated---5.605 chunks on average for DenseGraph and 6.305 for
HybridGraph---but less complete. Under this fixed budget, expansion is
displacing high-value seed evidence more often than it is recovering missing
context.

\subsection{Reranker Choice at a Fixed Candidate Pool}
\label{sec:ablation-reranking}

The third family provides the cleanest component comparison. It evaluates no
reranking, RRF-only, mMiniLM, Qwen3, BGE, and Jina over the same reconstructed
RRF pool of 30 candidates and retains the top 10. All six runs cover the same
500 questions with no retrieval or generation errors, and the reconstruction
matches the recorded RRF-only top-10 prefix for all 500 cases. Candidate
Recall@30 (0.5995) and Candidate Hit@30 (0.6450) are identical across
conditions, so the observed differences arise from ordering and retaining the
same available evidence.

Jina is the strongest retrieval configuration. Relative to RRF-only, it
improves Recall@10 by +9.23 pp, MRR@10 by +13.35 pp, and nDCG@10 by +11.85 pp;
all paired 95\% bootstrap intervals exclude zero. Qwen3 and BGE also show
positive retrieval effects, while mMiniLM delivers a smaller but reliable gain
at the lowest reranking cost. BGE has the highest observed Token F1 and
ROUGE-L, and Jina has the highest recorded unanswerable accuracy. These are
lexical and template-based diagnostics, however, not judgments of legal
correctness. Tables~\ref{tab:reranker-results} and~\ref{tab:reranker-paired}
give the aggregate and paired results.

\subsection{LLM Generator Ablation: Base versus CoT}
\label{sec:ablation-reasoning}
\label{sec:llm-reasoning-results}

This ablation asks whether a reasoning-oriented prompt changes observable
answer behavior when the model receives the same legal material. Every
condition uses the 500-question benchmark (400 answerable and 100
unanswerable questions), the same top-10 context, the
dense--graph--RRF--mMiniLM retrieval stack, temperature 0, and a 1,024-token
output limit. GPT-4o-mini Base versus CoT is the pre-specified primary
comparison: both runs complete all 500 cases and have identical ordered
top-10 chunk-ID sequences for every question.

The analysis reads frozen predictions, metrics, latency records, error files,
and manifests; it does not rerun retrieval or generation. Deltas are CoT minus
Base, with 95\% percentile confidence intervals from 10,000 paired bootstrap
resamples (seed 42). Exact Match also uses an exact McNemar test. Section~
\ref{sec:evaluation-metrics} defines the metrics and denominators.

\subsubsection{Primary paired result}

For GPT-4o-mini, CoT lowers Token F1 from 24.72\% to 23.15\% ($-1.57$ pp;
95\% CI [$-2.24,-0.92$]) and ROUGE-L from 21.60\% to 19.90\% ($-1.71$ pp;
95\% CI [$-2.34,-1.10$]). Both intervals exclude zero. Exact Match changes by
only $-0.25$ pp, with an interval that includes zero (McNemar $p=1.00$). CoT
wins, ties, and loses on Token F1 in 79, 164, and 157 cases, respectively:
the decline is broad but not universal. It also adds 0.76 seconds to median
generation and end-to-end latency, and 1.21 seconds to end-to-end p95 latency.

\subsubsection{Behavior conditional on available evidence}

Because retrieval is fixed, Recall, MRR, and nDCG are controls rather than LLM
outcomes. We instead group answerable cases by saved context Recall@10. CoT
lowers Token F1 when all gold support is present ($-1.98$ pp; $n=138$), when
support is partial ($-1.86$ pp; $n=29$), and when no gold support is retrieved
($-1.30$ pp; $n=233$). ROUGE-L changes in the same direction ($-2.30$, $-1.59$,
and $-1.37$ pp). Exact Match rises by 0.72 pp only in the fully retrieved
group; this small descriptive change does not reverse the primary result.
Evidence availability describes the context, not whether the answer is
faithful to it.

\subsubsection{Answerability decisions}

The saved field historically called \texttt{unanswerable\_accuracy} is a
template-based answer/refuse check over both answerable and unanswerable
questions. It is not a direct measure of refusal quality. Both GPT prompt
modes refuse 98 of the 100 unanswerable cases and answer the remaining two.
CoT's 1.40-pp gain in overall decision accuracy comes entirely from answering
seven more answerable cases, reducing template-detected false refusals from 85
to 78. The artifacts do not preserve a separate clarification decision, so the
result cannot establish whether the additional answers were appropriate.

\subsubsection{Citation structure, reliability, and descriptive model checks}

Table~\ref{tab:llm-reasoning-runs} consolidates all four models and both prompt
modes. For the primary GPT pair, CoT raises citation presence from 78.25\% to
81.00\% and mean unique sources from 1.46 to 1.68, while structural citation
coverage falls from 89.42\% to 87.91\%. Both runs have zero invalid markers.
These are formatting and placement diagnostics only; they do not show that a
cited passage entails the adjacent legal claim.

The other model pairs are descriptive checks, not additional primary tests.
Their CoT-minus-Base Token-F1 changes are $-1.33$ pp for
DeepSeek-V3.1-Thinking (95\% CI [$-1.96,-0.73$]), $-0.68$ pp for GLM-5
([$-1.23,-0.11$]), and $-1.92$ pp for Qwen3-8B ([$-2.77,-1.15$]). Four
DeepSeek cases have different ordered top-10 chunk sequences across prompts.
The Qwen manifests also differ in code snapshot, timeout, and retry limits, so
its latency and failure comparison is not strictly controlled. No prompt
effect is pooled across models.

\paragraph{Claim boundary.}
The saved artifacts contain final answers, lexical scores, retrieved contexts,
structural citation fields, and latency records. They do not contain legal
correctness labels, atomic-claim support labels, citation-entailment judgments,
preferred answer/clarify/refuse labels, raw chain-of-thought, or reasoning-token
accounting. The primary ablation therefore supports a narrow conclusion:
GPT-4o-mini CoT reduces template-detected false refusals and increases citation
presence, but lowers lexical overlap across all evidence-availability strata
and adds latency. It does not establish better or worse reasoning, legal
correctness, semantic grounding, citation entailment, or refusal quality.
