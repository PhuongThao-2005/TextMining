\section{Ablation Studies}
\label{sec:ablation-study}

This chapter follows the ablation plan in
\texttt{report\_structure.md}. We report five completed ablation analyses
grouped into four run families: embedding text; dense, hybrid, and graph
retrieval as one joint retrieval/graph family; reranking; and generator
prompting. Comparisons are made within a family because candidate
construction, context budgets, output limits, and latency accounting differ;
the results are not treated as one pooled factorial experiment.

\subsection{Embedding Text Ablation: Chunk-Only versus Chunk+Metadata}
\label{sec:ablation-chunk-meta}

This comparison holds the dense encoder, generator, benchmark, seed, and raw
FAISS evaluation path fixed. Only the indexed text changes from the chunk body
to a header-plus-chunk representation. On 400 paired answerable questions,
metadata leaves exact chunk Recall@10 effectively unchanged (48.5\% versus
48.5\%) but increases document-level success from 65.8\% to 70.2\%. The
paired document-success gain is +4.5 percentage points (95\% CI
[+0.5,+8.8]), whereas the MRR@10 change is $-5.9$ points (95\% CI
[$-9.1,-2.7$]). Metadata therefore improves document identification while
making the first exact gold chunk appear later; the complete scores are in
\hyperref[tab:embedding-results]{Table~\ref*{tab:embedding-results}} and the
retrieval curves and paired outcomes are shown in
\hyperref[fig:embedding-ablation]{Figure~\ref*{fig:embedding-ablation}}.

\subsection{Graph Ablation: With versus Without Graph Expansion}
\label{sec:ablation-graph}

Graph variants expand the top five seed chunks within their parent provisions
with \texttt{max\_hop=1}, replace the baseline top-10 context, and retain at
most 10 chunks. They use broad validity filtering without a query-time
validity overlay or reranking, so this comparison evaluates the complete
context-selection policy rather than graph traversal in isolation. Graph
expansion currently displaces more evidence than it adds.
Relative to DenseOnly, DenseGraph changes Recall@10 by $-12.40$ points and
loses at least one previously retrieved gold chunk for 61 questions while
adding a graph-only gold chunk for only one question. The HybridOnly to
HybridGraph change is $-11.81$ points, with 59 questions losing a gold chunk
and six gaining one; the displacement counts and rank deltas are summarized in
\hyperref[tab:graph-effect]{Table~\ref*{tab:graph-effect}}.

Graph contexts are denser because they contain fewer chunks on average (5.605
for DenseGraph and 6.305 for HybridGraph), and their identity precision is
higher. This is a precision--coverage trade-off, not evidence of an overall
retrieval-quality improvement. Of the 65 gold-chunk occurrences lost by
DenseGraph, 60 were outside the original top-five seeds; for HybridGraph, 52
of 60 were outside the original top five. The counts support seed-preserving
expansion as the next controlled graph test.

\subsection{Reranker Ablation}
\label{sec:ablation-reranking}

and Jina over a reconstructed shared RRF pool of 30 candidates and a final
top 10. All six runs cover the same 500 questions without retrieval or
generation errors, and the reconstruction matches the recorded RRF-only
top-10 prefix for 500/500 questions. Candidate Recall@30 is 0.5995 and
Candidate Hit@30 is 0.6450 for every configuration, so the comparison measures
ranking and retention within a fixed evidence ceiling.

\subsection{Generator Ablation: Base Prompt versus Chain-of-Thought Prompt}
\label{sec:ablation-reasoning}

\subsubsection{Control and pairing}

This ablation asks whether an explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompt changes
observable answer behavior when evidence retrieval is held fixed. The
pre-specified primary comparison uses GPT-4o-mini with the same benchmark,
saved retrieval stack, ordered top-10 context, citation contract, temperature
0, top-$p=1$, 1,024-token output limit, timeout, retry policy, and seed. Only
the prompt strategy and its prompt hash change between Base and CoT. The
post-hoc analysis reads the frozen predictions and does not rerun retrieval or
generation.
The observable prompt and output contracts are reproduced in
Appendix~\ref{app:prompt-output-formats}.

The manipulation is deliberately narrow. The Base template asks the generator
to answer directly and concisely from the supplied Vietnamese legal passages.
The CoT template adds an explicit instruction to inspect and connect the
evidence before returning the answer, while retaining the same refusal rule
and bracketed citation contract. Evaluation is applied to the visible final
answer only. The analysis neither assumes access to provider-side hidden
reasoning nor treats any exposed rationale as ground truth; therefore, ``CoT''
denotes the prompt condition rather than an observed faithful reasoning trace.

Both primary runs complete all 500 scheduled questions without failures or
skips. Their ordered top-10 chunk-ID sequences match for all 500 questions,
and all 400 answerable cases form valid lexical-metric pairs by
\texttt{qa\_id}; the remaining 100 questions are retained for answerability
diagnostics. This establishes a fixed-context comparison: retrieval metrics
are controls, not outcomes that the prompt can improve. The complete run
inventory is reported in
\hyperref[tab:llm-reasoning-runs]{Table~\ref*{tab:llm-reasoning-runs}}.

For scoring, the evaluator applies Unicode NFC normalization, lowercasing,
punctuation-to-space conversion, and whitespace collapse. Exact Match requires
equality after normalization; for Boolean questions it compares the first
non-empty line. Token F1 is the harmonic mean of precision and recall over
multiset token overlap, while ROUGE-L uses the longest common token
subsequence. All deltas below are computed per question as CoT minus Base.
Uncertainty is obtained from 10,000 paired bootstrap resamples with seed 42,
and the binary Exact Match transitions are additionally checked with an exact
McNemar test.

\subsubsection{Primary paired lexical result}

On the 400 paired answerable questions, CoT reduces Token F1 from 24.72\% to
23.15\%, a $-1.57$ percentage-point change whose 95\% paired-bootstrap
interval is $[-2.24,-0.92]$. ROUGE-L similarly decreases from 21.60\% to
19.90\% ($-1.71$ points, 95\% CI $[-2.34,-1.10]$). Both intervals exclude
zero. Exact Match changes only from 14.00\% to 13.75\% ($-0.25$ points, 95\%
CI $[-1.50,+1.00]$); its interval includes zero and the exact McNemar test
gives $p=1.00$.

The paired outcomes show that the aggregate decline is not caused by a small
number of extreme cases. CoT wins, ties, and loses on Token F1 for
79/164/157 questions and on ROUGE-L for 80/163/157 questions. Thus, losses
outnumber wins for both graded overlap metrics, whereas Exact Match is tied on
393 of 400 questions. Table~\ref{tab:llm-reasoning-paired} reports the means,
paired uncertainty, and per-question win/tie/loss counts.

\subsubsection{Evidence, answerability, citation, and latency diagnostics}

Conditioning on evidence availability does not reverse the primary result.
CoT Token F1 is lower when all annotated support is retrieved ($-1.98$
points, $n=138$), when support is partial ($-1.86$ points, $n=29$), and when
no gold chunk is retrieved ($-1.30$ points, $n=233$). ROUGE-L changes in the
same direction in all three strata. These are answer-overlap results
conditional on a fixed context, not measures of claim faithfulness; the full
breakdown is in
\hyperref[tab:llm-reasoning-evidence]{Table~\ref*{tab:llm-reasoning-evidence}}.

The saved template-based answerability diagnostic rises from 82.60\% to
84.00\%. This increase comes entirely from answering seven additional
answerable questions: false refusals fall from 85/400 (21.25\%) to 78/400
(19.50\%), while both prompts correctly refuse 98/100 unanswerable questions.
It therefore measures answer/refuse format behavior, not semantic refusal
quality. CoT also increases citation presence from 78.25\% to 81.00\% and
mean unique sources from 1.46 to 1.68, but structural citation coverage falls
from 89.42\% to 87.91\%. These citation fields test marker presence and parser
coverage rather than source entailment; see
\hyperref[tab:evidence-diagnostics]{Table~\ref*{tab:evidence-diagnostics}}.

The marker counts make the trade-off more concrete. CoT produces 714 parsed
citation markers versus 599 for Base, a 19.2\% increase, and neither condition
contains an invalid marker. Nevertheless, the lower structural coverage means
that adding markers and sources does not ensure that a larger share of scored
factual sentences is cited. The data therefore support a formatting claim
(more citation activity), not a grounding claim (better evidential support).

A supplementary length diagnostic computed from the frozen final answers also
shows that CoT changes response form. On the 400 answerable pairs, mean output
length rises from 43.98 to 52.66 whitespace-delimited tokens. The paired mean
increase is 8.69 tokens (95\% paired-bootstrap CI $[6.41,11.14]$); CoT is
longer on 176 cases, equal on 163, and shorter on 61. These are simple surface
tokens rather than provider billing or reasoning tokens. The longer output is
consistent with the additional instruction and may contribute to lower lexical
overlap, but the saved artifacts do not identify a causal mechanism.

The final column of Table~\ref{tab:llm-reasoning-runs} reports saved
end-to-end total p50/p95 latency, not generation time alone. Each percentile
uses all 500 rows in the corresponding \texttt{latency.json}; the millisecond
values were converted to seconds and cross-checked against
\texttt{run\_reliability.csv}. In the controlled GPT-4o-mini pair, generation
p50/p95 rises from 2.92/5.74 seconds to 3.68/6.17 seconds, while total p50/p95
rises from 13.99/17.21 seconds to 14.74/18.42 seconds. From the unrounded
artifacts, the total median and p95 increases are 0.759 seconds (5.4\%) and
1.213 seconds (7.0\%). The descriptive p50/p95 increases are 2.37/6.82 seconds
for DeepSeek, 1.67/2.13 for GLM-5, and 0.43/1.64 for Qwen3-8B; these secondary
pairs are not interpreted as prompt-only effects. The system-wide comparison
appears in \hyperref[tab:latency-results]{Table~\ref*{tab:latency-results}}.

\subsubsection{Descriptive model checks and claim boundary}

The other complete model pairs show the same descriptive direction for Token
F1: CoT minus Base is $-1.33$ points for DeepSeek-V3.1-Thinking, $-0.68$
for GLM-5, and $-1.92$ for Qwen3-8B. They are not pooled with the primary
effect. GPT-4o-mini was the pre-specified primary comparison; four DeepSeek
cases have different exact top-10 chunk-ID sequences, and the Qwen manifests
use different code snapshots, timeouts, and retry limits.

Under the controlled GPT-4o-mini setting, explicit CoT is therefore not a
demonstrated answer-quality improvement: it lowers both graded lexical-overlap
metrics and adds latency, while improving only template-based non-refusal and
citation-presence diagnostics. The artifacts contain no expert legal labels,
claim-level entailment annotations, raw chain-of-thought, reasoning-token
counts, or cost records. Accordingly, this ablation supports a conclusion
about observable output behavior under a fixed context, not about latent
reasoning quality, legal correctness, citation faithfulness, or deployment
safety.

\label{sec:evidence-analysis}

The GPT-4o-mini pair provides structural diagnostics for evidence availability,
answerability decisions, and citations. These fields describe observable
output format and saved parser coverage; they do not establish citation
entailment, semantic refusal quality, or legal correctness. CoT lowers the
false-refusal rate on answerable questions from 21.25\% to 19.50\%, while
unanswerable recognition remains 98.00\% for both prompts. Citation presence
rises from 78.25\% to 81.00\% and mean unique sources from 1.46 to 1.68, but
structural citation coverage falls from 89.42\% to 87.91\%; the detailed
diagnostics are in \hyperref[tab:evidence-diagnostics]{Table~\ref*{tab:evidence-diagnostics}}.

Evidence-stratified deltas do not reverse the result: CoT Token F1 is lower
when all gold support is retrieved ($-1.98$ points, $n=138$), when support is
partial ($-1.86$ points, $n=29$), and when support is absent ($-1.30$ points,
$n=233$). The saved records cannot support claim-level faithfulness.

\subsection{Latency and Efficiency Comparison}
\label{sec:ablation-efficiency}

Efficiency differences are substantial. Hybrid retrieval reports a one-time
BM25 precomputation cost of about 13,495 seconds, which contributes 26.990
amortized seconds per query to the reported hybrid totals, and a much larger
per-query retrieval cost. This batch accounting is not online request latency.
Among rerankers, mMiniLM is fastest, Jina is intermediate,
BGE is slower, and Qwen3 is much slower. The generator CoT prompt raises
generation p50/p95 from 2.92/5.74 seconds to 3.68/6.17 seconds, and total
p50/p95 from 13.99/17.21 to 14.74/18.42 seconds. Reranker p95, peak GPU
memory, and controlled full-online throughput are unavailable. The complete
stage and end-to-end comparison is linked in
\hyperref[tab:latency-results]{Table~\ref*{tab:latency-results}}.

\subsection{Qualitative Ablation Examples and Failure Cases}
\label{sec:qualitative-ablation}

The saved aggregate artifacts support a precise failure taxonomy even without
hand-labelled legal case studies. Metadata has a document--provision trade-off:
document-level success increases at top 10 while exact chunk MRR falls, so a
retrieved document title is not proof that the governing provision is ranked
early. Hybrid fusion can add lexical context while lowering exact gold-chunk
coverage; a generation-overlap gain is not evidence that RRF preserved the
gold ranked list. Graph expansion has a direct seed-displacement failure: it
introduces a new top-10 gold chunk for only 1/400 Dense and 6/400 Hybrid
questions, but removes at least one seed-retrieved gold chunk for 61 and 59
questions respectively. Finally, a fluent response with a structural citation
marker is not a verified legally correct or entailed answer.

Future case studies should preserve the \texttt{qa\_id}, gold chunk IDs,
ordered candidate IDs before and after the changed component, serialized
context, final answer, and citation parser output. A case must label whether
its diagnosis is an automatic rank fact or a human legal judgement; this keeps
illustrative examples auditable rather than anecdotal.

\subsection{Summary of Ablation Findings}
\label{sec:ablation-summary}

The five reported analyses support conditional engineering decisions. Metadata
helps document-level retrieval but not exact chunk ranking. The tested hybrid
configuration reduces exact-chunk coverage and adds latency. One-hop graph
expansion increases context density while displacing more gold evidence than
it adds. Cross-encoder reranking is the clearest positive retrieval result:
Jina gives the strongest ranking scores, BGE gives the strongest lexical
overlap, and mMiniLM gives the lowest recorded reranking cost. For the primary
generator pair, CoT lowers lexical overlap under fixed context while improving
only template-based decision diagnostics and adding latency. The consolidated
recommendations are linked in
\hyperref[tab:ablation-decision-summary]{Table~\ref*{tab:ablation-decision-summary}}.
