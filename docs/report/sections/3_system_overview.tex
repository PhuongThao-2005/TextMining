\section{Legal Dataset and Benchmark Construction}
\label{sec:dataset-construction}

The experiments use two linked data products: a processed Vietnamese legal
corpus and a question-answer benchmark with explicit evidence provenance. The
ingestion path preserves the hierarchy of an instrument instead of flattening
all text into unrelated passages. This section records the transformations,
quality gates, and final statistics needed to reproduce the evaluation
denominators.

\subsection{Legal Corpus and Raw Data Sources}
\label{sec:raw-corpus}

The raw corpus is derived from the Hugging Face dataset
\href{https://huggingface.co/datasets/th1nhng0/vietnamese-legal-documents}
{\texttt{th1nhng0/vietnamese-legal-documents}}
\citep{ngo2026vietnameselegal}. According to its dataset card, the corpus was crawled from the
\href{https://vbpl.vn}{Vietnamese Government Legal Document Portal}
using Scrapy. The project uses three upstream configurations:
\texttt{metadata}, \texttt{content}, and \texttt{relationships}, containing document metadata, raw HTML bodies, and directed document-to-document relations, respectively.

For reproducibility, all experiments use a frozen local snapshot processed by dataset pipeline version \texttt{2.0.0} and reconciled on 2026-07-06. The snapshot includes metadata, HTML content, and relationship records. Relationship labels originate from the source portal metadata and are not generated by an LLM.

The snapshot is not treated as a live legal service. All corpus-size and reconciliation counts are reported once in Section~\ref{sec:dataset-validation}; they describe the frozen evaluation package and do not imply complete coverage or current legal validity of all Vietnamese legal instruments.

\subsection{Dataset Normalization and Cleaning}
\label{sec:dataset-normalization}

Normalization converts heterogeneous source records into deterministic fields before structuring and indexing. Source identifiers are preserved as strings and used consistently across metadata, content, relationship, provision, chunk, and graph artifacts. Text fields are Unicode-normalized to NFC and cleaned using deterministic whitespace rules without free-form rewriting. Portal dates in \texttt{DD/MM/YYYY} format are converted to ISO \texttt{YYYY-MM-DD}; missing values remain null, while malformed or invalid dates are recorded as quality flags.

Document types are mapped to a controlled legal-document vocabulary and to seven authority ranks, while validity metadata is normalized into canonical status groups such as \texttt{active}, \texttt{expired}, \texttt{partial}, \texttt{suspended}, \texttt{future}, and \texttt{unknown}. Issuing authority, legal field, sector, and scope are likewise mapped to versioned vocabularies while preserving their original source values for auditability.

Raw HTML is not embedded directly. Content records sharing the same document identifier are deterministically merged, hashed, and converted to normalized text using \texttt{bs4+lxml+cleanrules@1}. The pipeline retains provenance information and quality flags so that extraction and normalization decisions can be audited. The main normalization rules are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:normalization-rules}.

Cleaning is intentionally conservative. Unresolved relationships are retained as auditable references rather than silently discarded. Records with missing required metadata, unknown document type, invalid issue-date conditions, quarantined relationship endpoints, duplicate relationship triples, or self-loops are excluded from the accepted corpus and recorded in the corresponding quarantine ledger.

\subsection{Relationship Processing and External References}
\label{sec:relationship-processing}

Relationship processing uses the source-provided \texttt{relationships} records from the portal crawl.
Each record identifies a source document, a target document, and a Vietnamese relationship label.
The pipeline normalizes document identifiers and maps source labels to a canonical relation vocabulary while preserving the original record for provenance.

These source records define only document-to-document legal relations such as citation, amendment, replacement, suspension, and related-document links. Containment and reading-order edges are constructed deterministically by the text-structuring layer: \texttt{DOCUMENT\_HAS\_PROVISION} links each accepted document to its parsed provisions, \texttt{PROVISION\_HAS\_CHUNK} links each provision to its emitted chunks, and \texttt{PROVISION\_NEXT}/\texttt{CHUNK\_NEXT} connect siblings in source order. The report therefore distinguishes source legal relations from derived structural graph edges.

Graph edges follow the convention that
\texttt{src\_id -[REL]-> dst\_id}
reads as ``source REL target.'' Relation groups whose direction can be deterministically resolved from the source semantics, including citation, guidance, supplementation, amendment, and related-document relations, are marked as direction-verified. Relations involving replacement, validity, or suspension remain explicitly unverified when their direction cannot be established reliably from the available metadata. Such edges are retained for audit but are not treated as authoritative directional evidence by downstream graph reasoning.

Targets that resolve to accepted corpus documents are linked directly to their canonical document identifiers.
References to documents outside the frozen corpus are represented as \texttt{ExternalStub} nodes rather than fabricated retrievable documents.
These stubs preserve the existence and provenance of external references while remaining excluded from normal text retrieval.

The resulting relation inventory supports both structural context recovery and cross-document dependency analysis. Authority and temporal-validity reasoning is applied only when the required metadata and relation direction have passed the corresponding verification checks.

\subsection{Quarantine and Data-Quality Verification}
\label{sec:quarantine}

Quality verification is applied before records enter the retrieval index or graph.
The pipeline checks metadata completeness, document type and date validity, structural parentage, text availability, relationship endpoints, duplicate edges, self-loops, and relation-direction status.
Failed metadata or relationship records are written to quarantine ledgers with explicit reason tags. Missing or unusable body text is recorded separately in \texttt{text\_provenance.jsonl} and produces no retrievable provisions or chunks.

Unresolved external targets are not treated as quarantine failures; they are retained as \texttt{ExternalStub} records. Similarly, relations with unverified direction remain auditable but are excluded from authoritative directional reasoning, while unknown validity metadata is preserved rather than inferred.

This conservative policy favors traceability over corpus size: records with unreliable metadata or structure are excluded from retrieval rather than silently repaired. The resulting coverage trade-off is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:discussion-limitations-ethics}.

\subsection{Legal Text Structuring}
\label{sec:text-structuring}

The corpus follows the hierarchy
\textit{Document $\rightarrow$ Provision $\rightarrow$ Chunk}.
Documents retain source-level metadata, provisions represent citable legal units such as articles, items, sections, and preambles, and chunks are the units indexed for retrieval. Chunk-level retrieval metrics are therefore evaluated against \texttt{chunk\_id} identifiers.

Chunking is provision-bounded: text from different provisions is never merged into the same chunk. Provisions whose extracted text does not exceed the hard limit remain as a single chunk. The pipeline uses a maximum of approximately 1,200 estimated tokens, corresponding to \texttt{CHUNK\_MAX\_CHARS = 4,800} under the deterministic estimate of four characters per token.

Longer provisions are split within their parent provision. The splitter first prefers legal and visual boundaries such as \textit{khoản}, numbered paragraphs, list items, and table-like breaks. When these boundaries are insufficient, it falls back to a paragraph-aware sliding-window strategy targeting approximately 900 estimated tokens per chunk with about 130 tokens of overlap. All emitted chunks remain subject to the 1,200-token hard maximum.

Document titles, provision headings, and citation metadata are retained at the parent levels rather than duplicated into the base chunk record. They are joined with chunk text only when constructing metadata-enriched retrieval representations. Stable document, provision, and chunk identifiers preserve provenance throughout indexing, retrieval, and evaluation.

The final structured corpus contains 1,386,267 provisions and 1,513,376 chunks. This representation allows retrieved chunks to be matched directly to benchmark evidence while preserving their originating provision and document. It also enables neighboring context to be recovered without losing source provenance.
\subsection{QA Benchmark Construction}
\label{sec:benchmark-construction}

The benchmark contains 500 Vietnamese legal questions: 400 answerable questions with reference evidence and 100 questions verified as unanswerable within the frozen corpus. Benchmark generation is implemented in \texttt{notebooks/archive/gen-qa.ipynb}. The pipeline samples seed provisions from the frozen v2 corpus, constructs related evidence bundles containing up to five provisions, and uses \texttt{gemini-3.1-flash-lite} to generate user-style Vietnamese legal question-answer pairs.

The generation prompt provides the selected evidence bundle, a code-defined generation plan, and structured output constraints. It discourages trivial document-centric questions and instead targets practical legal information needs. A separate generation path produces validity and cross-document cases involving amendment history, partial validity, and interactions between legal instruments.

Unanswerable items are represented as an answer type rather than a separate question category. They are generated by requesting plausible legal details for which no supporting evidence is available in the frozen corpus, such as a specific amount, office, form, procedure, or condition. To verify these cases, the notebook gives the verifier the candidate question, the proposed insufficient-context answer, and the evidence bundle from which the question was generated, then requires a pass only when the evidence is insufficient and the empty gold set is appropriate. The frozen-corpus claim is operational: within the benchmark pipeline, no supporting document, provision, or chunk identifier from the frozen corpus is assigned, and the verifier confirms that the supplied evidence does not answer the question. It is not a human legal proof that no possible support exists outside the frozen snapshot. Their reference answer is normalized to ``Không có đủ thông tin trong đoạn luật.'', and their document, provision, and chunk gold identifiers are empty by construction. In the final benchmark, the 100 unanswerable items consist of 93 single-hop, 5 legal-validity, 1 cross-document, and 1 multi-hop questions.

Verification is performed in a separate model call using \texttt{gemini-3.1-flash-lite}. Each candidate is checked against its evidence for answer correctness, evidence sufficiency, unsupported claims, category consistency, and difficulty consistency. A candidate is rejected if any required verification criterion fails. The verifier also assigns one of three difficulty labels: \texttt{easy}, \texttt{medium}, or \texttt{hard}.

\subsection{Question Categories and Answer Types}
\label{sec:question-taxonomy}

Question categories describe the evidence pattern required to answer a question. \textit{Single-hop} questions rely on one local passage; \textit{citation} questions require identifying the relevant legal authority; \textit{legal-validity} questions depend on temporal status or validity; \textit{multi-hop} questions require multiple linked provisions; and \textit{cross-document} questions require evidence spanning related legal instruments.

Each benchmark item is assigned exactly one primary category. When multiple labels are plausible, the most diagnostic evidence pattern is selected, prioritizing legal-validity, cross-document, multi-hop, citation, and then single-hop.

Answer type is recorded separately as \textit{abstractive}, \textit{boolean}, \textit{extractive}, or \textit{unanswerable}. Difficulty is also independent of category and answer type.
The verifier assigns \texttt{easy} to local single-fact cases, \texttt{medium} to questions requiring multiple facts, linked provisions, cross-document evidence, or validity reasoning, and \texttt{hard} to cases involving interacting conflict or partial-validity signals.

\subsection{Ground-Truth Evidence}
\label{sec:ground-truth}

Each benchmark item stores its question, category, answer type, reference answer, and a structured evidence object containing document, provision, and chunk identifiers:

\begin{equation}
Q_i =
\left(
\mathrm{qa\_id},
\mathrm{question},
\mathrm{category},
\mathrm{answer\_type},
\mathrm{reference\_answer},
\mathrm{ground\_truth}
\right).
\end{equation}

For each answerable question, the gold chunk set $G_i$ is the relevance unit used for Recall@$k$, Hit@$k$, MRR@$k$, and nDCG@$k$. Document and provision identifiers are retained to support document-level and structural analyses in addition to exact chunk ranking. For unanswerable questions, $G_i=\varnothing$ by construction and these cases are excluded from retrieval-metric denominators.

Gold identifiers are derived from code-defined generation plans rather than free-form model output. For answerable questions, the generation plan specifies the supporting provision identifiers. A minimal-evidence tagger then maps these provisions to chunk-level evidence partly by retaining chunks whose token overlap with the reference answer is at least 0.25. If no chunk satisfies this threshold, all chunks belonging to the planned provision are retained to avoid assigning an answerable question an empty gold set. A validation step rejects answerable records that lack document, provision, or chunk identifiers, after which the verifier checks that the reference answer is supported by the supplied evidence.

The term \textit{gold evidence} is used operationally for the benchmark's reference identifiers rather than as expert-adjudicated legal ground truth. Because chunk assignment partly depends on reference-answer token overlap, alternative provisions or broader authority sets may also support a valid answer, but the automatic retrieval metrics credit only the identifiers included in the benchmark.

\subsection{Dataset Statistics and Validation}
\label{sec:dataset-validation}

Before export, the pipeline reconciles source, normalized, structured, and
indexed counts. Every document points to zero or more provisions; every
indexed chunk points to one provision and one document; every resolved legal
edge points to existing endpoints; and every external reference points to a
stub rather than an invented target. Missing-text, orphan, quarantine, and
unknown-validity counts are retained in the audit ledger even when the final
retrieval index excludes them.

The validation identities are:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{raw}} &= N_{\mathrm{accepted}} + N_{\mathrm{quarantined}},\\
N_{\mathrm{accepted\_relationships}} &= N_{\mathrm{resolved}} + N_{\mathrm{external\_target}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
