\section{Legal Dataset and Benchmark Construction}
\label{sec:dataset-construction}

The experiments use two linked data products: a processed Vietnamese legal
corpus and a question-answer benchmark with explicit evidence provenance. The
ingestion path preserves the hierarchy of an instrument instead of flattening
all text into unrelated passages. This section records the transformations,
quality gates, and final statistics needed to reproduce the evaluation
denominators.

\subsection{Legal Corpus and Raw Data Sources}
\label{sec:raw-corpus}

The corpus contains Vietnamese legal instruments such as National Assembly
laws, government decrees, Prime Minister decisions, and ministry circulars.
Each source record is associated with a stable document identifier, title,
issuing authority, issue and effective dates, validity status, document type,
and the raw body from which provisions and chunks are derived. Relationship
records retain their source and target identifiers, relation type, and any
available date or confidence metadata.

The processed inventory contains 151,624 documents, 1,386,267 provisions,
1,513,376 retrieval chunks, 883,256 document-level relationships, and 19,763
external-reference stubs. The scale summary is linked in
\hyperref[tab:corpus-statistics]{Table~\ref*{tab:corpus-statistics}}. The
counts refer to processed records, not a claim that every current Vietnamese
instrument is present or legally valid.

\subsection{Dataset Normalization and Cleaning}
\label{sec:dataset-normalization}

Normalization converts heterogeneous source records into deterministic fields.
Identifiers are canonicalized before joins; dates are represented in one
machine-readable convention; document type and status values are mapped to a
controlled vocabulary; and empty or malformed body fields are retained in an
audit stream instead of silently becoming empty passages. The normalization
rules are summarized in \hyperref[tab:normalization-rules]{Table~\ref*{tab:normalization-rules}}.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\scriptsize
\caption{Normalization rules applied before indexing.}
\label{tab:normalization-rules}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.24\columnwidth}p{0.64\columnwidth}}
\toprule
\textbf{Field} & \textbf{Rule and audit behavior} \\
\midrule
Identifier & Trim, canonicalize separators, and preserve the source key for traceability. \\
Dates & Parse issue/effective dates into a consistent date representation; retain missing values for audit. \\
Document type & Map source labels to a controlled type vocabulary; keep the raw label as metadata. \\
Status & Preserve effective, amended, repealed, and unknown states rather than inferring validity from text. \\
Text & Normalize whitespace and headings while retaining the original body and provision order. \\
Relations & Resolve in-corpus targets; create an external stub when the target is unavailable. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Cleaning is intentionally conservative. A record is not discarded merely
because it has an unresolved relation, since that relation can be useful as a
visible limitation. Records with unusable identifiers, missing structural
parents, or failed parsing gates are quarantined and counted separately from
the accepted corpus.

\subsection{Relationship Processing and External References}
\label{sec:relationship-processing}

Relationship processing separates resolved in-corpus targets from references
that point outside the snapshot. Resolved edges store a canonical source,
target, relation vocabulary value, direction, and provenance. An unresolved
target becomes an \texttt{ExternalStub} with the original reference string;
the stub is not treated as retrievable legal text. Direction is checked against
the relation semantics before a graph edge is made available to query-time
traversal.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\scriptsize
\caption{Relationship vocabulary used by the graph-ready corpus.}
\label{tab:relationship-vocabulary}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.38\columnwidth}p{0.49\columnwidth}}
\toprule
\textbf{Relation} & \textbf{Interpretation} \\
\midrule
\texttt{DOCUMENT\_HAS\_PROVISION} & Document contains a hierarchical provision. \\
\texttt{PROVISION\_HAS\_CHUNK} & Provision owns an indexed passage. \\
\texttt{CHUNK\_NEXT} & Adjacent chunks in reading order. \\
\texttt{PROVISION\_NEXT} & Adjacent provisions in reading order. \\
\texttt{CITES} & Source instrument explicitly cites a target instrument. \\
\texttt{AMENDS}/\texttt{REPEALS} & Source changes or terminates a target instrument. \\
\texttt{REPLACES} & Source supersedes a target instrument. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The relation inventory supports both structural context recovery and legal
dependency analysis. It does not turn every citation into a validity decision:
authority and effective-date overlays are applied only when the relevant
fields and edge direction have passed verification.

\subsection{Quarantine and Data-Quality Verification}
\label{sec:quarantine}

Quality verification runs before a record contributes to the retrieval index
or graph. The gates check identifier uniqueness, document-to-provision and
provision-to-chunk parentage, non-empty indexed text, relation endpoint
resolution, and direction consistency. A failed gate writes the record and
reason to a quarantine ledger so that the final inventory can be reconciled
without guessing how many records were removed.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\scriptsize
\caption{Data-quality gates and their downstream effect.}
\label{tab:data-quality-gates}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.34\columnwidth}p{0.53\columnwidth}}
\toprule
\textbf{Gate} & \textbf{Action on failure} \\
\midrule
Identifier and parent integrity & Quarantine; do not emit a retrievable child record. \\
Text availability & Quarantine empty or unparseable passages; retain audit reason. \\
Relation endpoint & Resolve target or emit an external stub. \\
Relation direction & Keep in audit stream until direction is verified. \\
Validity metadata & Preserve unknown status and exclude it from strict filters. \\
Count reconciliation & Block export until document/provision/chunk counts agree. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

This quarantine policy is conservative by design. It avoids propagating an
incorrect legal edge, but it can lower recall by excluding records whose
metadata is incomplete. That trade-off is part of the corpus limitations
discussed in Section~\ref{sec:discussion-limitations-ethics}.

\subsection{Legal Text Structuring}
\label{sec:text-structuring}

The canonical hierarchy is
\textit{Document $\rightarrow$ Provision $\rightarrow$ Chunk}. A document
stores source-level metadata; a provision stores article, clause, item, and
heading information; and a chunk stores the text presented to the retriever,
its sequence number, and pointers back to both parents. The embedded text can
be chunk-only or metadata-enriched, but the provenance fields remain present
in both variants.

For each indexed passage, the minimum provenance object is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{ChunkMeta} = \{&\mathrm{chunk\_id},\mathrm{doc\_id},\\
&\mathrm{provision\_id},\mathrm{title},\\
&\mathrm{issuing\_authority},\mathrm{effective\_date},\\
&\mathrm{sequence}\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This structure lets evaluation match retrieved IDs to gold evidence even when
the visible passage is shortened for a prompt. It also lets the graph restore
neighboring passages without losing the source document.

\subsection{QA Benchmark Construction}
\label{sec:benchmark-construction}

The benchmark contains 500 Vietnamese legal questions: 400 answerable
questions with gold evidence and 100 deliberately unanswerable questions.
Candidates were generated from bundles of approximately three to five related
provisions and chunks using \texttt{gemini-3.1-flash-lite}. A separate
verification pass checked answerability from the supplied evidence, absence
of unsupported claims, category and difficulty labels, and consistency of the
reference answer. Diversity filtering and deduplication were applied only
after candidates passed those checks.

The final generation run sampled 515 documents and 1,697 provisions, and
72.41\% of candidates passed verification before filtering. The resulting
question records carry the same stable \texttt{qa\_id} through retrieval,
generation, and post-hoc analysis. Unanswerable questions have empty gold
evidence by construction so that refusal behavior can be measured separately
from answer generation.

\subsection{Question Categories and Answer Types}
\label{sec:question-taxonomy}

Question categories encode the evidence pattern rather than the expected
answer length. Single-hop questions can be answered from one local passage;
citation questions require identifying a named authority; legal-validity
questions depend on status or date; multi-hop questions require more than one
linked passage; and cross-document questions require a relation between
instruments. Answer types are abstractive, boolean, extractive, or
unanswerable. The final category counts are linked in
\hyperref[tab:benchmark-taxonomy]{Table~\ref*{tab:benchmark-taxonomy}}.

\subsection{Ground-Truth Evidence}
\label{sec:ground-truth}

Each question stores its text, category, answer type, reference answer, and a
ground-truth object containing the supporting document, provision, and chunk
identifiers:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Q_i=\{&\mathrm{qa\_id},\mathrm{question},\\
&\mathrm{category},\mathrm{answer\_type},\\
&\mathrm{reference\_answer},\mathrm{ground\_truth}\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For answerable questions, the gold chunk set $G_i$ is the retrieval relevance
unit used for Recall@$k$, Hit@$k$, MRR@$k$, and nDCG@$k$. The document and
provision IDs are retained so that document-level success and structural
coverage can be analyzed alongside exact chunk ranking. For unanswerable
questions, $G_i=\varnothing$ by design; they are not silently inserted into
retrieval denominators.

Gold evidence is an annotation target, not a complete legal theory. A legal
expert may regard an alternative provision or a broader authority set as
sufficient, and the benchmark does not provide that semantic judgment. The
evaluation therefore states exactly what the gold IDs measure and what they do
not establish.

\subsection{Dataset Statistics and Validation}
\label{sec:dataset-validation}

Before export, the pipeline reconciles source, normalized, structured, and
indexed counts. Every document points to zero or more provisions; every
indexed chunk points to one provision and one document; every resolved legal
edge points to existing endpoints; and every external reference points to a
stub rather than an invented target. Missing-text, orphan, quarantine, and
unknown-validity counts are retained in the audit ledger even when the final
retrieval index excludes them.

The validation identity is checked at each stage:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{input}} &= N_{\mathrm{accepted}} + N_{\mathrm{quarantined}},\\
N_{\mathrm{relations}} &= N_{\mathrm{resolved}} + N_{\mathrm{external}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
These identities make the corpus summary in
\hyperref[tab:corpus-statistics]{Table~\ref*{tab:corpus-statistics}} auditable.
They also explain why a graph or strict validity filter can reduce available
evidence: the system prefers a visible missing or uncertain record to an
unverified relation presented as authoritative.
