\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This controlled ablation study identifies where L\_RAG currently gains value
and where extra machinery is not yet justified. Metadata-enriched embeddings
improve document-level coverage but not exact-chunk ranking. The tested hybrid
fusion and one-hop graph expansion reduce exact-evidence coverage while adding
cost or displacing strong seed passages. Cross-encoder reranking is the
consistent exception: with the candidate pool fixed, Jina improves Recall@10,
MRR@10, and nDCG@10 over RRF-only with paired confidence intervals above zero.
BGE leads the observed lexical overlap metrics, while mMiniLM has the lowest
reranking cost.

The fixed-context generator ablation adds an important qualification. For
GPT-4o-mini, CoT prompting lowers Token F1 by 1.57 pp and ROUGE-L by 1.71 pp,
improves only template-based answerability diagnostics, and increases latency.
It is therefore not a demonstrated improvement in legal-answer quality.

Taken together, the results support a dense-retrieval-plus-reranker baseline,
with Jina as the current quality--latency candidate for this benchmark. They
do not establish legal correctness, citation faithfulness, or safe autonomous
legal advice: the available artifacts lack human semantic evaluations.

\subsection{Future Work}
\label{sec:future-work}

The next phase should add blinded expert labels for legal correctness,
completeness, claim faithfulness, citation precision, and citation recall. It
should also measure full-online p95 latency, GPU memory, and throughput;
tune candidate-pool size, RRF, and graph budget; and validate graph relation
direction against authoritative document pairs. The most promising technical
change is seed-preserving graph expansion, which can add surrounding context
without discarding high-confidence retrieved evidence. Future prompt studies
should rerun all conditions with provenance-aligned manifests and evaluate
answers, citations, and abstentions semantically rather than through lexical
or template-based proxies.
